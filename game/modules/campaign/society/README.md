# Civil society, media and public institutions

**Owner:** game/modules/campaign/society. **Status:** design only; no society/civilian runtime exists.

Own campaign-level civilian population, households, community institutions, media and public consequence projections. World owns physical cities/buildings; logistics owns commodities and delivery; justice/political policy records own decisions; tactical systems publish events and estimates. Society must not rewrite tactical truth.

## Population and displacement
Track population by city/region/cohort where warranted, with explicit source and confidence. Model evacuation, displacement, refugees, returns, shelters, family separation, public services and population resilience. Civilian exposure depends on location, infrastructure/service availability, weather/hazards and recorded conflict events. Preserve identities only where the game requires a named individual; do not create meaningless millions of per-person entities.

## Public institutions
Use a common institution record (mandate, capacity, budget/resources, trust, service area, history) for municipal councils, hospitals/clinics, emergency services, civil defence, schools/universities, utilities, relief organisations, heritage bodies and veterans' organisations. Projects may include shelters, food banks, clinics, missing-person desks, search/rescue, rebuilding and heritage protection. Waste/sanitation backlog, power/water outages and overloaded hospitals have downstream community effects.

## Media and public information
Fictional outlets can have ownership, reach, editorial policy, verification practice, censorship pressure and journalist safety state. They report events from evidence available to them, distinguish confirmed/estimated/reported/contested claims and may issue corrections. Different outlets may frame the same event differently, but repetition never upgrades a claim into truth. Public trust and resilience influence bounded morale/policy effects.

## Culture and justice interfaces
Heritage sites, museums, religious shelters, sports/cultural venues and tourism may be protected, evacuated, suspended, damaged or resumed. Police, courts, civil claims, criminal cases, curfews and corruption are owned by a separate justice-policy subdomain where detailed enough; this page defines the civilian effects and links to those records.

## Human-cost rules
Civilian deaths, injuries, displacement, disrupted services and destroyed homes are consequences, not score bonuses. Distinguish direct observations from aggregate estimates. Memorials and histories consume the authoritative event/personnel record; they do not turn casualties into achievements.

## Validation
Population accounting and uncertainty; evacuation/shelter capacity; service outage propagation; relief delivery; conflicting media reports and corrections; censorship/journalist risk; public trust; heritage/culture state; no campaign side effect from renderer-only damage.
