# 14 — Electronic Attack and Radar Protection

## Purpose
Describe hostile emissions and deliberate interference, and how radar systems detect, mitigate, or work around them.

## Categories
- **Noise jamming:** barrage, spot, swept, and other noise-like interference, including power and geometry.
- **Deceptive jamming:** range-gate pull-off, velocity-gate pull-off, false-target generation, and related deception concepts.
- **Repeater/coherent interference:** delayed or modified copies of radar emissions and their effects on processing.
- **Self-protection/escort jamming:** platform and external jammer relationships, geometry, and coordination.
- **Burn-through and geometry:** relative range, power, antenna direction, and processing; avoid one universal burn-through distance.
- **ECCM:** frequency agility, sidelobe control, adaptive processing, waveform changes, and documented protections.
- **Sidelobe/mainlobe interference:** distinguish interference entering through different antenna directions/channels.
- **Interference detection:** noise rise, degraded signal conditions, suspicious returns, and possible deception.
- **Emission control/passive operation:** radar silence, intermittent emissions, and passive-sensor coordination.
- **Friendly/co-site interference:** nearby radars, datalinks, adjacent bands, and electromagnetic compatibility.

## Interactions
Jamming enters the signal environment and receiver/processing chain as interference. ECCM imposes costs and limitations; it does not guarantee immunity.

## Boundary
Covers radar response to electronic attack and electromagnetic interference. General platform ECM equipment and threat libraries may be shared with the broader EW subsystem.
