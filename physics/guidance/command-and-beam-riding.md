# Command guidance and beam riding

**Owner:** physics/guidance, with the missile seeker/receiver and launcher or director installed by the weapon/platform packages. **Status:** architecture specification only.

These architectures keep an external guidance source in the loop. They are not interchangeable with self-contained homing.

## Command guidance

A ground/ship/aircraft director estimates the target and sends steering commands or updates to the weapon. A command link may be radio, wire/fibre or another documented channel. The weapon need not measure the target itself; it follows commands using its own navigation and control state.

Model director source, track quality, command update cadence, latency, quantization, line-of-sight, link range, packet loss/interference, weapon receiver state, command authority, fail policy and control limits. The director's track is an uncertain estimate, not campaign truth. If the link or director is lost, follow the weapon's documented coast, inertial continuation, last-command, terminal fallback or failure behavior.

Distinguish MCLOS (operator commands missile manually), SACLOS (operator tracks target/aimpoint while system forms steering commands), and automatic command guidance. Do not treat these labels as a missile seeker type.

## Beam-riding guidance

A beam-rider measures its position relative to a beam/reference axis generated by an external director and steers to remain within the beam. The beam may be radar RF or laser energy depending on the actual system. The weapon's receiver measures beam-relative error; it is not automatically homing on energy reflected by the target.

Model director line-of-sight and beam pointing error, stabilization, beam divergence/spread with range, spatial energy profile, sidelobes/false references where relevant, propagation/attenuation and obscuration; weapon receiver field of regard, sensitivity, processing/update and angular error; director-target tracking continuity, beam interruption, masking, terrain/structure obstruction; and weapon steering authority, response lag and control limits.

A radar beam rider and a laser beam rider share an abstract beam-reference contract but require different propagation, receiver, atmospheric and interference models. Laser beam-riding uses optical propagation/obscuration; radar beam-riding uses RF propagation and receiver behavior. A lost/ambiguous beam means loss or degradation of the beam reference, not a guaranteed hit.

## Separation from other guidance

- Beam riding: weapon follows a reference beam.
- Command-to-line-of-sight: director calculates and sends commands to keep the weapon on a commanded trajectory/LOS.
- Semi-active homing: weapon measures energy illuminated/reflected by the target and homes on the return.
- Active homing: weapon transmits and receives with its own active terminal sensor.

These methods can be combined with inertial midcourse, but their information sources remain explicit.

## Validation

Test beam centering; director/weapon/target motion; beam divergence and receiver limits; beam interruption and reacquisition; LOS masking; datalink loss; command age; control saturation; and deterministic replay. Validate radar and laser variants separately.
