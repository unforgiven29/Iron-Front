# Aerodynamic flow interactions and aircraft-specific effects

**Owner:** physics/flight-model/flow-interactions for selecting and configuring aircraft interaction models; reusable local-flow physics belongs to physics/aerodynamics and environment. **Status:** architecture only.

A-4E-C's FM contains an LERX-related component and optional spline-driven visual effects alongside its main element calculations. This points to a general design need: some aircraft depend on flow interactions that cannot be represented by independent isolated panels alone. Iron-Front must keep physical interaction logic separate from the renderer and select it only when data and validation support it.

## Interaction families

Potential model capabilities include:
- Downwash/sidewash and wake from upstream surfaces.
- Vortex lift and vortex breakdown, including LERX/LEX or strake effects.
- Propeller slipstream/propwash or rotor downwash.
- Jet exhaust interaction and powered-lift effects.
- Wing/fuselage/tail interference and local shielding.
- Ground effect and surface-normal influence near runways/decks.
- High-AoA cross-flow, separated-flow and nonlinear control effectiveness.
- Store/pylon/pod interference and inlet-distortion interaction where supported.

Not every aircraft needs every interaction. A fixed extension or strake is geometry; its vortex behavior is a model, not a visual animation.

## Modeling tiers

Start with isolated panel/local flow. Add empirical correction surfaces only when supported by test/reference data. More detailed vortex/wake or unsteady models require explicit validity envelope, temporal state, convergence/stability evidence and browser budget. Never add arbitrary multipliers to force one aircraft to match an anecdotal maneuver.

## Presentation boundary

Vapour, condensation, smoke, contrails and streamlines are visualization outputs derived from physical state. They do not create lift, drag, stall or sensor detection. Visual spline opacity or particle count must never be used as an input to flight forces.

## Tests

Test interaction activation range, sign/magnitude against reference data, ground height/surface dependence, control response with and without interaction, mirrored asymmetry, high-AoA boundaries, temporal stability and cost scaling with number of surfaces.


## Dynamic vortex, wake and airframe-flow interaction

Use this owner to configure which interaction models an aircraft actually has. Reusable velocity-field and aerodynamic response mathematics belong to the corresponding physics owners. The aircraft package chooses geometry, model profile, sources and validity envelope.

### Distinguish the flow structures

Do not call every visible swirl a "vortex" and give it the same behavior.

- **Leading-edge/LEX/LERX vortex:** generated by sharp/swept leading-edge or forebody geometry under suitable incidence; can augment lift, change side force and alter fin/tail/inlet flow. Strength, path and breakdown depend on exact geometry and state.
- **Wingtip vortex:** trailing vortex associated with finite-wing lift distribution; contributes induced velocity/drag and aircraft wake.
- **Trailing vortex sheet / spanwise loading:** distributed wake behind lifting surfaces, not a single fixed point emitter.
- **Aircraft wake encounter:** time-evolving vortex structures shed by another aircraft; convect, spread and decay in the environment before affecting receiving surfaces.
- **Propulsive flow:** engine jet or propeller slipstream; has separate velocity, temperature and pressure characteristics and is owned through propulsion/flow contracts.
- **Separated-flow structures:** local separation/reattachment and dynamic-stall vortex near a moving/high-incidence section; state belongs to unsteady aerodynamics and is not identical to a persistent LEX vortex.

### Dynamic field representation

A reduced-order browser model may represent selected coherent structures as finite-core vortex filaments/segments, vortex particles or another documented approximation. A field record should contain:
- stable structure ID, source aircraft/entity and originating surface;
- circulation/strength estimate and sign convention;
- core radius/regularization and spatial extent;
- origin, orientation, segment endpoints or compact support;
- convection velocity and sampled ambient wind;
- age, decay/diffusion state and validity;
- optional breakdown/instability state when supported;
- source model, fidelity tier and uncertainty.

Use regularized finite-core behavior; an ideal zero-radius singularity is not suitable for real-time simulation. Never move a receiving aircraft directly to create an upset. The receiving aircraft samples induced velocity at its own aerodynamic elements, then its own local aero model computes force and moment.

### LEX/LERX and forebody interaction

For each aircraft, separately define the fixed geometric source, its active flow envelope, affected surfaces and known downstream interactions. Possible outputs include:
- local induced-flow vector at each affected element;
- local incidence/sidewash changes;
- pressure/load distribution modifiers;
- tail/fin effectiveness changes and buffet excitation where supported;
- inlet-face distortion input where physically connected;
- breakdown onset/location only if validated.

For the MiG-29, its LERX/root and forebody geometry must be modeled as its own flow source. For the F-16, do not borrow F/A-18 or modified high-AoA F-16 results as production Block 50 vortex calibration. Include only its actual strake/forebody geometry and model profile supported by evidence. A visual condensation trail is a render product, not the vortex state.

### Multi-aircraft wake interaction

Wake state belongs to the world/flow simulation, not to the renderer or to one aircraft's private data. The previous committed wake field influences the current tick; new wake is emitted after current aircraft loads/motion are resolved. This avoids update-order dependence where the first aircraft updated in a tick cannot be affected by another aircraft updated later.

Define:
- wake emission criteria and source loading state;
- convection by ambient wind and wake-induced velocity where modeled;
- descent/roll-up, diffusion/decay and ground interaction at declared fidelity;
- spatial indexing and query radius;
- max structures/segments and deterministic retirement;
- receiver sampling at aerodynamic element points;
- uncertainty and quality as wake ages or enters an unsupported region.

Do not let every aircraft query every vortex structure. Use spatial acceleration and bounded time/space lifetimes. If the wake budget is exceeded, degrade through an explicit documented policy that reports dropped/coarsened structures; do not silently change the aircraft's base aerodynamic coefficients.

### Interactions with other systems

- Unsteady aerodynamics consumes local induced velocity and owns section separation/dynamic-stall state.
- Aeroelasticity consumes load distributions and may respond to wake/buffet excitation.
- Compressibility may alter vortex/shock interaction only through an explicit validated model.
- Propulsion-integration may consume inlet-distortion fields; engine internals remain in physics/propulsion.
- Sensors may use actual wake/thermal/RF environment only through their own physical propagation paths.
- Graphics renders smoke/condensation/streamlines from state; visuals never feed back into physics.

### Validation

Test vortex sign and induced-velocity direction, finite-core behavior, circulation/decay trend, field continuity, convection and wind, LEX/LERX geometry sensitivity, tail/fin/inlet sampling, two-aircraft wake encounter, ground proximity if supported, breakdown limits, order-independent deterministic replay and performance scaling. Compare against the relevant NASA flow visualization/velocity surveys in the advanced reference guide, but do not transfer their research-configuration values to either production aircraft.
